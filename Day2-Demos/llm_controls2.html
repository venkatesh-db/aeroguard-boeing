The context window: the model's working memory

An LLM can only "see" a limited number of tokens at once: your instructions, the chat so far, and any documents. When it's full, the oldest content falls out.

💡 Bigger windows let you paste whole manuals, but they cost more (you pay for every token sent, every time) and are slower. Models can also miss details buried in the middle of very long inputs. That is why techniques like RAG send only the relevant pages.
Page counts are rough (≈ 500 tokens per page). Exact limits vary by model and change often.

💡 Temperature reshapes the bars: low makes the top word dominate, high flattens them. Top-p cuts off the long tail: at 0.5, only the smallest set of words covering 50% of the probability can be picked (greyed-out words are banned). Usually you adjust one of them, not both.

Big, medium or mini: which model for the job?

Bigger models are smarter but slower and pricier. Pick a task and a daily volume, and see which model fits best.

💡 Rule of thumb: start with the smallest model that passes your quality test, and move up only for tasks that need deeper reasoning. A mini model can be roughly 10–20× cheaper than the largest tier, which adds up fast at high volume.
